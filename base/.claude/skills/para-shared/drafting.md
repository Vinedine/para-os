# Drafting for the operator (shared across skills)

Read before drafting anything the operator will paste and send: a mail, a message, a letter.

1. **Name what it answers**: the thread and the message, by sender and date, or the meeting record it follows, by its date, above the draft.
2. **Real recipients.** To and CC are addresses from the thread, the meeting's calendar invite (a recorder note's attendee list is one) or the contact card in `areas/network/`, never one said or written in a meeting's notes or transcript, a bare name, a placeholder or a group ("the team"). Look an unknown one up in each before asking for it.
3. **The channel's format.** A mail body is plain text, no Markdown: no asterisks, headings or bracketed links. One paragraph is one unbroken line, never hard-wrapped, with a blank line between paragraphs; only a real list item gets a line of its own.
4. **Nothing the vault does not hold.** No price, date or commitment that neither the thread nor the vault contains: mark the gap inline, in the sentence that needs it (`[date: not in the vault]`).
5. **A complaint gets an acknowledgement**, not a promise.
6. **Follow the house format.** A proposal, quote or letter of a kind the vault already holds follows the structure of the latest sent instance, not its line breaks; name any section you would add.
7. **No reason, no draft.** With nothing in the vault saying why to write now, ask for the reason instead of drafting generic copy.
8. **In the sender's voice.** Where a `.claude/rules/voice-*.md` profile names the account the draft goes out from, read it and follow it: the greeting and sign-off for the thread's language, the tone for this recipient, none of its never-uses phrases. [voice-profile.md](voice-profile.md) builds one when asked and turns a correction into a line.
